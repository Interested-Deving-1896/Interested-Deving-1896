# The Continuity Ledger — OSP fictional folklore

> This is fictional OpenOS-Project-OSP folklore, not a description of real
> finances, contributors, organizations, or OpenCollective activity. For actual
> contribution options and terms, use the
> [OpenOS Project OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/openos-project/contribute).

In OSP folklore, the Continuity Ledger is kept in the Continuity Harbor. Every
entry identifies what must remain available, why it matters, what resources it
may use, and what evidence will show that the work was completed.

Relay appears through the **Continuity Prism**, carrying outcome records between
the harbor's workshops. Nexus appears as the **Mirror Keeper**, checking that a
mission has a visible origin, a bounded purpose, and a defined stopping point.
Neither character owns the ledger or grants secret access.

## The four continuity circles

The circles are narrative metaphors. They are not current contribution products,
prices, ranks, entitlements, or promised benefits.

### Harbor Lights

Small signals of support illuminate routine maintenance: documentation,
accessibility, integrity checks, testing, and recovery instructions. A Harbor
Light never encodes a hidden message or purchases preferential treatment.

### Builder Rings

Builder Rings represent recurring support for visible operational work such as
hosting, release infrastructure, mirror verification, contributor tooling, and
public learning resources. Every ring has a written scope and review date.

### Continuity Constellations

Continuity Constellations represent larger, milestone-bound resilience work:
reproducible releases, recovery capacity, accessibility infrastructure,
replaceable services, and cross-forge continuity. Funding remains separate from
technical authority, security access, and governance.

### Open Commissions

An Open Commission is a custom proposal for a bounded public benefit. It names
the objective, eligible costs, decision process, deliverables, risks, evidence,
and completion or retirement conditions before work begins.

It is never a blank cheque, private auction, protection payment, or covert
contract. A proposal involving secrecy, coercion, retaliation, concealed
beneficiaries, or unlawful activity cannot enter the Continuity Ledger.

## From shadow trope to visible continuity

| Rejected shadow trope | OSP folklore transformation |
|---|---|
| Payment as coded signal | Public purpose, issue reference, and readable change record |
| Sponsorship as protection | Recovery and incident-response work governed by common rules |
| Hidden institutional payroll | Scoped grants, declared conflicts, milestones, and reporting |
| Custom contribution as auction | Open proposals evaluated against published criteria |
| Financial power as control | Separation of funding, maintainership, governance, and access |
| Permanent obligation | Review dates, stop conditions, and a retired state |

The story's challenge is not whether the Mirror Keeper can be trusted
personally. It is whether the Continuity Harbor remains inspectable when
resources, urgency, and influence grow.

## Continuity-entry template

| Entry | Story function |
|---|---|
| Scope | Names the OSP workshop or shared operational route |
| Objective | Describes the continuity outcome sought |
| Eligible costs | Defines which operational resources may be supported |
| Allocation | Shows how resources are assigned without obscuring provenance |
| Cadence | Establishes one-time, recurring, or milestone-based support |
| Evidence | Identifies the public outcome record returned by Relay |
| Dependencies | Makes approvals, thresholds, partners, and constraints visible |
| Status | Marks the mission proposed, active, paused, fulfilled, or retired |

## Canon boundaries

- This folklore makes no allegation about any real contribution or contributor.
- It assigns no criminal, intelligence, military, governmental, or covert role
  to OpenOS-Project-OSP, OpenCollective, or any person.
- It makes no claim about a fiscal host's legal or tax status.
- Live names, amounts, availability, and terms are intentionally excluded.
- Operational documentation, financial records, licenses, and governance rules
  always override fictional language.

The Continuity Ledger makes one OSP principle memorable: resilience is credible
only when purpose, provenance, verification, and recovery remain visible.
