# Creative provenance

This character system grew from several conversations about giving the
Interested-Deving-1896, OSP, and OOC relationship a memorable creative layer.
It records influences openly while keeping the published canon original.

## Ideation record

The initial discussion included the public Google Gemini conversation
[The Ultimate Unified Persona Profile](https://gemini.google.com/share/7eb1ddf414b2),
created on 25 September 2026 and most recently published on 6 October 2026.
The canonical `gemini.google.com/share/` URL is used because the shorter
`share.gemini.google` aliases can change when the conversation is republished.
That conversation is an ideation record, not
Mirrorchain canon and not a factual description of any person or project.

The conversation combined traits from existing fictional characters and used
real organizations in speculative scenarios. This project does not reproduce
those characters, relationships, scenarios, names, costumes, or claims.
Instead, it transforms broad themes into original open-source values:

| Early theme | Mirrorchain interpretation |
|---|---|
| Hidden logistics | Transparent synchronization and provenance |
| Control | Consent-based coordination and user autonomy |
| Intelligence networks | Observability, documentation, and shared knowledge |
| Defense | Resilience, recovery, and threat modelling |
| Closed networks | Federated open-source communities |

## OpenCollective tier addition

The discussion was later extended with the
[OpenOS Project OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/openos-project) and
used a four-part contribution-tier pattern for fictional worldbuilding. Its
response described covert payments, extortion, unlawful markets, and hidden
institutional influence. Those scenarios are neither factual claims nor
Mirrorchain canon.

The [Stewardship Ledger](lore/STEWARDSHIP-LEDGER.md) preserves only the useful
narrative structure: small community support, recurring infrastructure support,
larger milestone-bound stewardship, and custom public-benefit proposals. It
reverses the original shadow-economy premise into transparent scope,
accountability, consent, separation of funding from authority, and explicit
stop conditions.

Live OpenCollective tier names, prices, availability, legal status, and
financial terms are not reproduced in the lore. The OpenCollective listing and
applicable operational records remain authoritative.

## Digital-sovereignty addition

The discussion was later extended with
[KDE's digital-sovereignty guidance](https://kde.org/for/digital-sovereignty/).
Gemini used it to give the Sovereign Nexus concept an ideological dimension,
contrasting public commitments to autonomy and decentralization with a
fictional risk of replacing visible lock-in with hidden central control.

Mirrorchain adopts the useful design test, not the control fantasy: users must
remain able to inspect their systems, self-host their data, choose independent
providers, replace components, and migrate through open standards. No project,
vendor, maintainer, or fictional character should hold all the keys.

## Community-character influence

KDE's [Konqi character documentation](https://community.kde.org/Konqi) and
[mascot artwork library](https://community.kde.org/Promo/Material/Mascots)
demonstrate how an open-source community can maintain character traits,
worldbuilding, reference artwork, contextual variants, and community
contributions.

Mirrorchain borrows that organizational idea, not KDE's protected identity or
visual design. Relay is not a KDE dragon, and Mirrorchain artwork must not use
KDE logos, antlers, scarves, silhouettes, or copied character art.

## Expanded Sovereign Nexus ensemble

A later user-provided Markdown export expanded the fictional persona into **The Sovereign Nexus**, with the optional narrative alias **Elias "Cipher" Vance**. The ideation blended broad archetypal influences from action, espionage, detective, science-fiction, fantasy, legal, and superhero stories. Additional named influences included Henry "Fitz" Fitzgerald (*Ghost Soldier*), Landon Kirby, Frank Castle, and Matt Murdock.

The expanded material also explored fictional analogies involving OpenOS Project, OSP, OOC, KDE digital sovereignty, OpenCollective, Canadian public institutions, CSIS, the Canadian Armed Forces, NATO, and the United Nations. Those thriller premises included covert control, unlawful surveillance, sabotage, money laundering, manipulation of public institutions, and violence. They are fictional tropes only; they are not factual claims about any person, project, platform, government, military, intelligence service, or institution.

The updated [Nexus character study](cosplay/NEXUS.md) retains the useful creative themes—strategic patience, technical imagination, psychological insight, diplomacy, honor, protective loyalty, resilience, due process, and moral restraint—while explicitly rejecting hidden control and unlawful conduct as project values. Real organizations are described only through verified public roles, and no affiliation or covert relationship is implied.

The character's central design test is practical digital sovereignty: inspectability, user choice, portability, revocable permissions, independent review, transparent funding, separated authority, and safe exit paths. The Phoenix motif becomes tested recovery rather than invulnerability; the tactical-protector motif becomes incident readiness and care for affected people; and the Stewardship Ledger remains a fictional transparent public-benefit framework, not a description of real OpenCollective tiers or transactions.

AI-assisted drafts and fan-inspired concepts remain subject to human review for originality, factual accuracy, accessibility, attribution, and clear fiction labelling.
