# The Stewardship Ledger — fictional tier folklore

> This is fictional project folklore, not a description of real finances,
> people, contributors, organizations, or OpenCollective activity. For actual
> contribution options and terms, use the
> [OpenOS Project OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/openos-project/contribute).

In Mirrorchain folklore, the Stewardship Ledger is a public book kept between
the Source Workshop, the Continuity Harbor, and the Open Constellation. Every
entry names its purpose, destination, limits, and evidence before any resource
begins its journey.

Nexus appears here as the **Ledger Weaver**, translating community intentions
into finite, reviewable missions. Relay carries each entry between the three
workshops and returns with an outcome record. Neither character owns the
ledger, grants secret access, or decides alone how resources are used.

## The four circles

These circles adapt the four-part tier pattern from the Gemini ideation record.
They are narrative metaphors, not current OpenCollective products, prices, or
promised benefits.

### The Ember Circle

Small signals of support gather like lights around the Source Workshop. In the
story, they sustain documentation, accessibility work, testing, and ordinary
community maintenance.

An Ember never encodes a secret message. Its purpose is stated openly, and no
contribution purchases influence, preferential treatment, or private access.

### The Builder Rings

The Builder Rings represent recurring support for visible infrastructure:
hosting, release engineering, continuity checks, contributor tooling, and
public learning resources.

Each ring is attached to a written scope and review date. Recognition may thank
a supporter, but it cannot become an endorsement, governance seat, or shield
from the same rules that apply to everyone else.

### The Steward Constellation

The Steward Constellation represents larger, milestone-bound support for work
that benefits more than one Mirrorchain layer. Examples include accessibility
infrastructure, reproducible release systems, recovery capacity, open
standards, and independently replaceable services.

No institution gains hidden control by supporting a constellation. Funding is
separated from technical authority, and each mission records dependencies,
conflicts, allocation, and public evidence appropriate to the work.

### The Open Commission

The Open Commission represents a custom proposal for a clearly bounded public
benefit. A commission begins with an open description of the objective,
eligible costs, decision process, deliverables, risks, and completion or
retirement conditions.

It is not a blank cheque or private auction. Proposals that require secrecy,
coercion, retaliation, concealed beneficiaries, or unlawful activity never
enter the Stewardship Ledger.

## From shadow trope to open stewardship

The Gemini draft framed its fictional tiers as parts of a covert economy.
Mirrorchain deliberately reverses those devices:

| Rejected shadow trope | Mirrorchain transformation |
|---|---|
| Payments as coded dead drops | Public purposes, issue references, and readable change records |
| Sponsorship as protection | Resilience and incident-response work available under common rules |
| Hidden institutional payroll | Scoped grants, declared conflicts, milestones, and public reporting |
| Custom contribution as auction | Open proposals evaluated against published criteria |
| Financial power as control | Separation of funding, maintainership, governance, and security access |
| Permanent obligation | Review dates, stop conditions, and an explicit retired state |

The tension remains useful for storytelling: Nexus must continually prove that
an open ledger has not become a new center of invisible control. The answer is
never personal virtue alone. It is inspectable process, distributed authority,
clear consent, and the ability for participants to leave or replace a layer.

## A ledger-entry template

Every fictional mission in this lore uses the same fields as the public README
tier template:

| Entry | Story function |
|---|---|
| Scope | Names the Source Workshop, Continuity Harbor, Open Constellation, or a shared route |
| Objective | Describes the public outcome sought |
| Eligible costs | Defines what resources may support |
| Allocation | Shows how shared resources travel between layers |
| Cadence | Establishes whether support is one-time, recurring, or milestone-based |
| Evidence | Identifies the outcome record that returns with Relay |
| Dependencies | Makes approvals, thresholds, partners, and constraints visible |
| Status | Marks the mission proposed, active, paused, fulfilled, or retired |

## Canon boundaries

- This folklore makes no allegation about any real contribution or contributor.
- It does not assign criminal, intelligence, military, governmental, or covert
  activity to OpenCollective, OpenOS Project, OSP, OOC, or any person.
- It makes no claim about a fiscal host's legal or tax status.
- Live contribution names, amounts, availability, and terms are intentionally
  excluded because the OpenCollective page is authoritative.
- Operational documentation, financial records, licenses, and governance rules
  always override fictional language.

The Stewardship Ledger exists to make one narrative principle memorable:
resources strengthen the Mirrorchain only when their purpose and path remain
visible.
